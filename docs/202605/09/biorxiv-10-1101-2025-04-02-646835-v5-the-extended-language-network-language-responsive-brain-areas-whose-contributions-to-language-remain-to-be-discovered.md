## 摘要
尽管语言神经科学主要关注左侧额叶与颞叶的核心脑区及其右半球同源区，但众多其他皮层与皮层下区域也被认为与语言处理有关。然而，由于相关证据来自多种实验范式——这些范式往往将语言处理与感知、运动或任务相关的认知过程混杂在一起——这些区域对语言的具体贡献仍不明确。利用772名被试（438名女性、334名男性）的fMRI数据，这些被试执行了一种经过广泛验证的语言定位范式，该范式能够将语言处理与其他过程区分开，我们：（a）描绘出一组在书面与听觉模式下对语言稳定响应的脑区；（b）评估这些脑区相对于高负荷非语言任务的语言选择性。与以往的观点一致，许多位于核心额颞网络之外的区域在语言处理中表现出响应，并且大多数区域相对于一般任务需求显示出语言选择性。这些扩展语言网络中的语言选择性区域包括颞极周围区域、内侧额叶皮层、海马体以及小脑等。尽管分布于脑的多个部分，扩展语言选择性网络仍仅占灰质体积的一小部分（<5%），这一发现对“整脑皆参与语言处理”的观点提出了挑战。这些新识别出的语言选择性区域现可被系统地加以表征，以阐明其对语言处理的具体贡献，并检验这些贡献是否有别于核心语言区。

## Abstract
Although language neuroscience has largely focused on core left frontal and temporal brain areas and their right-hemisphere homotopes, numerous other areas - cortical and subcortical - have been implicated in linguistic processing. However, these areas contributions to language remain unclear given that the evidence for their recruitment comes from diverse paradigms, many of which conflate language processing with perceptual, motor, or task-related cognitive processes. Using fMRI data from 772 participants (438 females, 334 males) performing an extensively validated language localizer paradigm that isolates language processing from other processes, we a) delineate a comprehensive set of areas that respond reliably to language across written and auditory modalities, and b) evaluate these areas selectivity for language relative to a demanding non-linguistic task. In line with prior claims, many areas outside the core fronto-temporal network respond during language processing, and most of them show selectivity for language relative to general task demands. These language-selective areas of the extended language network include areas around the temporal poles, in the medial frontal cortex, in the hippocampus, and in the cerebellum, among others. Although distributed across many parts of the brain, the extended language-selective network still only comprises a small fraction (<5%) of the grey matter volume, challenging the view that the entire brain processes language. These newly identified language-selective areas can now be systematically characterized to decipher their contributions to language processing, including testing whether these contributions differ from those of the core language areas.
