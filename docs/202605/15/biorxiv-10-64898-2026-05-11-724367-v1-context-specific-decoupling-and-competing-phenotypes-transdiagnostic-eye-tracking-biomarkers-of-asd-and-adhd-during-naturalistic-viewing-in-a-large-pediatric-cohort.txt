Abstract 23 Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) and Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) show 24 considerable clinical overlap, yet categorical diagnostic boundaries can obscure shared 25 physiological vulnerabilities. To characterize these traits dimensionally and categorically, we 26 analyzed multimodal eye-tracking synchrony in a large-scale transdiagnostic pediatric cohort 27 from the Healthy Brain Network during naturalistic viewing ( N = 2,036). Using a novel two-28 dimensional framework that simultaneously captures the strength and spatial geometry of gaze 29 alignment across subjects — alongside a measure of shared pupillary responses — we 30 quantified how closely children's visual attention and physiological arousal matched those of 31 their typically developing peers across four distinct media contexts. When autistic and ADHD 32 traits were modeled concurrently, their effects on physiological alignment proved context-33 specific: autistic traits were uniquely associated with reductions in visual attention and arousal 34 synchrony during a complex social narrative, whereas ADHD traits independently predicted 35 reduced synchrony during fast-paced, visually demanding media. Examining the spatial 36 direction of gaze misalignment further revealed that each child tended to scan the scene along a 37 distinct spatial axis, inconsistent with a uniform group-level processing shift. Categorical models 38 additionally revealed a non-additive interaction in comorbidity: rather than compounding 39 physiological deficits, the attentional flexibility characteristic of ADHD appeared to partially offset 40 the restricted spatial attention associated with isolated ASD, resulting in comparatively less 41 severe gaze decoupling in the comorbid group. These findings support the utility of a Research 42 Domain Criteria (RDoC) framework, positioning model-free physiological synchrony as a 43 candidate transdiagnostic biomarker and suggesting that comorbid ASD and ADHD reflect 44 competing rather than simply additive neurocognitive strategies. 45

1. Introduction 47 Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) and Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) 48 are highly prevalent neurodevelopmental conditions with substantial phenotypic and 49 genetic overlap. Although historically conceptualized as distinct entities, their clinical co-50 occurrence is exceptionally high; up to 70% of autistic individuals exhibit clinically 51 significant ADHD symptoms. These populations share overlapping neurocognitive 52 profiles characterized by atypical spatial visual attention, executive dysfunction, and 53 socio-cognitive difficulties (1,2). Despite this overlap, traditional diagnostic frameworks 54 (e.g., DSM-5) partition these conditions into rigid categorical boundaries. While 55 necessary for clinical triaging, this approach masks mechanistic heterogeneity and limits 56 the examination of comorbid interactions. To address these limitations, the Research 57 Domain Criteria (RDoC) framework (3,4) advocates mapping continuous symptom 58 dimensions—such as social communication (via the Social Responsiveness Scale; SRS; 59 (5)) and attention (via the SWAN scale; (6))—onto objective, transdiagnostic 60 physiological biomarkers. 61 Historically, capturing visual and attentional differences in ASD and ADHD relied 62 on static, highly controlled paradigms that often lack ecological validity. Consequently, 63 the field has increasingly adopted naturalistic viewing (e.g., movie-watching), which 64 provides a continuous, multi-sensory stream that naturally engages sustained attention 65 and social cognition (7). During naturalistic viewing, eye movements and pupillary 66 arousal offer moment-to-moment readouts of cognitive and affective engagement. To 67 decode these complex time series without relying on predefined computational models, 68 researchers increasingly utilize Inter-Subject Correlation (ISC) (8,9). By using the 69 empirical consensus of typically developing peers as a model-free baseline, ISC 70

Consequently, it remains unclear 77 whether neurodivergent populations exhibit a consistent, systematic viewing bias 78 shared across individuals, or highly idiosyncratic spatial locking onto non-normative 79 features. 2.3. Eye-Tracking and Pupillometry Acquisition 144 While simultaneous electroencephalography (EEG) was recorded during the paradigms, only 145 eye-tracking and pupillometry data are analyzed here. Gaze position and pupil diameter were 146 acquired using an infrared iView-X RED-m eye tracker (SensoMotoric Instruments GmbH) 147 operating at 30, 60, or 120 Hz. The system has a reported spatial resolution of 0.1° and gaze 148 position accuracy of 0.5°. 149 Prior to each video, a standardized 5-point grid calibration and validation procedure was 150 performed. Calibration was repeated until the spatial error was <2° for any single point and the 151 average spatial error was <1°, ensuring high-fidelity spatial trajectories and accurate pupillary 152 measurements for subsequent analysis. 153 154 

2.4. Eye-Tracking and Pupillometry Preprocessing 155 Raw data were preprocessed via a custom Python pipeline to isolate valid physiological signals. 156 Horizontal gaze, vertical gaze, and pupil diameter time series were extracted. Invalid data points 157 (zero or missing) were classified as blinks or hardware tracking loss. Blink periods were 158 symmetrically padded by ~33 milliseconds, adjusted for native sampling rates, to account for 159 partial eyelid closures. 160 Stringent quality control thresholds were enforced: participants missing >10% of their 161 total data after blink padding were entirely excluded. For retained participants, short gaps ( ≤500 162 ms) were linearly interpolated. Time series were downsampled to 30 Hz and truncated to video 163 length, yielding standardized, temporally aligned matrices for ISC analysis. 164 165

2.6. Statistical Analysis 199 All statistical analyses were conducted in Python using the statsmodels library. To preserve the 200 context-specific nature of the naturalistic viewing paradigms, models were computed separately 201 for each of the four video stimuli, with missing data handled via listwise deletion. Participant age 202 and biological sex were included as covariates in all models. To control for multiple comparisons 203 across viewing contexts, Benjamini-Hochberg False Discovery Rate (FDR) corrections ( q-values) 204 were applied independently within each physiological metric family (Gaze ISC Magnitude, 205 Absolute Gaze Phase, and Pupil ISC). 206 

2.6.1. Dimensional (RDoC) Analysis 207 To evaluate continuous, transdiagnostic relationships between symptom severity and 208 physiological synchrony, we employed multiple linear regression models. All dependent 209 variables (Gaze ISC Magnitude, Absolute Gaze Phase | θ|, Pupil ISC) and primary independent 210 variables (SRS Total T-scores and SWAN Total scores) were within-video standardized (Z-211 scored) prior to modeling. This allowed the resulting regression coefficients ( β) to be interpreted 212 as standardized effect sizes. Three modeling tiers were executed: 213 

• Separate Models (Domain-General): SRS and SWAN scores were modeled independently 214 to assess the broad impact of autism and ADHD traits. 215 

• Joint Models (Specificity): SRS and SWAN scores were entered simultaneously, forcing 216 the traits to compete for variance to isolate effects unique to specific trait domains. 217

3.5. Sensitivity Analyses: Clinical Severity, Outliers, and Medication Confounds 360 To ensure the observed interactive effect between ASD and ADHD diagnosis - whereby 361 comorbid ADHD symptoms appeared to attenuate the spatial decoupling seen in isolated ASD -362 represented a robust neurodevelopmental phenotype rather than a statistical or 363 pharmacological artifact, we conducted three targeted sensitivity analyses (for full statistical 364 parameters, see Supplementary Section S2 and S3). 365 First, we evaluated a clinical severity confound—the possibility that the severe spatial 366 decoupling uniquely observed in the isolated ASD group was driven by a more profound core 367 autism phenotype. Notably, the ASD without ADHD group presented with significantly milder 368 parent-reported autism traits (SRS Total) than the comorbid group ( M = 65.96 vs. M = 70.15, p =369 0.003). This suggests that the comorbid group’s lower decoupling is not explained by milder 370 autism severity, supporting a mechanistic buffering effect driven by concurrent ADHD traits. 371

Cross-referencing our analytical sample with the HBN DailyMeds tracker 379 revealed that active stimulant administration on the day of scanning was negligible (only 25 380 confirmed cases, representing 6.44% of the medication-tracked subsample, and at least 1.23% 381 of the full analytical sample assuming zero stimulant use among the untracked majority; 0 cases 382 in the ASD without ADHD group and 5 in the comorbid group). Furthermore, incorporating 383 medication status as a covariate in the ANCOVAs did not materially alter the magnitude or 384 significance of the interaction effects. Acute stimulant administration is unlikely to explain the 385 primary findings, although medication effects cannot be fully excluded. 386 387 

4. Discussion 388 The present study utilized a large-scale pediatric cohort to map continuous and categorical 389 dimensions of ASD and ADHD onto the physiological rhythms of naturalistic viewing. By 390 employing a 2D Complex Correlation and 1D Pupillary ISC framework, we quantified the spatial 391 magnitude, absolute phase divergence, and autonomic entrainment of neurodivergent youth. 392 Our findings reveal three core principles. First, physiological decoupling is profoundly context-393 specific: autistic traits are uniquely associated with dyssynchrony during complex social 394 narratives, whereas ADHD traits associated with dyssynchrony during high-salience, fast-paced 395 media. Second, absolute phase mapping revealed that spatiotemporal misalignment is highly 396 idiosyncratic; rather than exhibiting a uniform developmental processing delay or systematic 397

4.1. Context Dictates the Biomarker: Social vs. Salience-Driven Decoupling 403 Our joint dimensional models suggest that a single context-agnostic biomarker may be 404 insufficient. When SRS and SWAN traits were placed in direct statistical competition, the locus 405 of visual and autonomic dyssynchrony shifted dramatically depending on the cognitive and 406 emotional load of the stimulus. 407 Autistic traits uniquely drove widespread spatial and pupillary dyssynchrony during The 408 

Present . This narrative requires continuous mentalization of the characters' emotional states. 409 The profound decoupling of gaze and autonomic arousal observed aligns closely with the social 410 motivation hypothesis (11), which posits a fundamental reduction in the rewarding value of 411 social stimuli. Crucially, this socio-emotional decoupling is not simply a categorical deficit but 412 scales continuously with dimensional trait severity. 413 In stark contrast, ADHD traits independently drove physiological dyssynchrony during 414 

Despicable Me —a fast-paced animation dominated by intense bottom-up sensory competition. 415 While children with high SWAN scores maintained normative synchrony during slower narratives, 416 their physiological entrainment fractured under rapid visual shifts. This likely reflects core 417 deficits in sustained attention and an exaggerated vulnerability to bottom-up perceptual 418 distraction (12). These double dissociations suggest a critical methodological imperative: 419 extracting robust biomarkers relies entirely on pairing the target condition with the appropriate 420 stimulus context. 421 422 

4.2. Fractured Rhythms: Absolute Phase and Idiosyncratic Spatial Locking 423 

> .CC-BY-NC-ND 4.0 International license available under a (which was not certified by peer review) is the author/funder, who has granted bioRxiv a license to display the preprint in perpetuity. It is made The copyright holder for this preprint this version posted May 15, 2026. ;https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.11.724367 doi: bioRxiv preprint

18 A critical innovation of this study was deploying a 2D Complex Correlation framework to 424 disentangle the overall strength of visual entrainment from absolute spatial-temporal 425 misalignment (phase offset, | θ|). Historically, theories regarding ASD and ADHD have frequently 426 posited generalized deficits in processing speed or universal "developmental lags" (13,14). 427 Our analyses did not support a uniform delay hypothesis. Because absolute phase 428 captures the angular misalignment between a subject's and the normative group's dominant 429 axes of 2D gaze variation, elevated | θ| values indicate that neurodivergent children are not 430 simply 'trailing behind'—they are tracking the visual scene along a systematically different 431 spatial orientation. Critically, the large variance is consistent with substantial individual 432 heterogeneity. This is broadly consistent with prior reports of heterogeneous neural responses in 433 neurodivergent populations (15,16). The visual rhythms of neurodivergent children are not 434 simply delayed; they are uniquely and idiosyncratically fractured. 435 Furthermore, evaluating these traits in direct statistical competition revealed a critical 436 distinction between visual engagement (magnitude) and spatial misalignment (phase). In the 437 isolated dimensional models, both SRS and SWAN severity significantly predicted absolute 438 phase divergence (| θ|) during specific paradigms, such as Despicable Me and Diary of a Wimpy 439 

Kid . However, in the joint models, these phase effects were entirely attenuated. This loss of 440 unique predictive power suggests that idiosyncratic spatial tracking is likely driven by 441 generalized neurodevelopmental variance shared across the ASD and ADHD spectra, rather 442 than being uniquely attributable to either trait in isolation. This contrasts sharply with Gaze ISC 443 Magnitude—where autistic traits retained unique explanatory power during social narratives— 444 further emphasizing that the strength of visual entrainment and the spatial geometry of that 445 entrainment are governed by distinct neurocognitive mechanisms. 446 447 

4.3. Competing Phenotypes: The Antagonistic Interaction of Comorbidity 448 

> .CC-BY-NC-ND 4.0 International license available under a (which was not certified by peer review) is the author/funder, who has granted bioRxiv a license to display the preprint in perpetuity. It is made The copyright holder for this preprint this version posted May 15, 2026. ;https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.11.724367 doi: bioRxiv preprint

19 While dimensional models isolated unique trait vulnerabilities, the 2×2 factorial analysis 449 exposed how ASD and ADHD interact within the individual. Historically, research has assumed 450 comorbid ASD and ADHD constitutes a 'double hit' (1). However, our spatiotemporal data offer a 451 different pattern. 452 Visual decoupling in the comorbid group is not an additive sum of isolated deficits. 453 Instead, the concurrent presence of ADHD was associated with higher spatial synchrony than 454 the ASD without ADHD group—despite the comorbid group carrying greater overall clinical 455 burden. As demonstrated by our sensitivity analyses (Section 3.5), this effect was not driven by 456 baseline severity differences, outliers, or pharmacological normalization from stimulant use. 457 This pattern may reflect competition between distinct visual strategies. Isolated ASD 458 frequently involves 'sticky attention'—rigid spatial fixation on idiosyncratic, non-social features 459 (17,18). Without competing attentional dynamics, normative synchrony collapses. Conversely, 460 the hyperscanning and impulsive gaze-shifting characteristic of ADHD (19) may interrupt this 461 rigid spatial locking, increasing the frequency of gaze transitions that incidentally overlap with 462 the normative trajectory. In the comorbid phenotype, these two opposing tendencies may partly 463 offset one another at the level of the observed synchrony metrics. Direct validation through 464 fixation density and saccadic frequency mapping remains an important future direction. 465 Interestingly, because these antagonistic buffering effects were absent in the continuous trait 466 interaction models (Table S1), this compensatory dynamic may only emerge at distinct clinical 467 diagnostic thresholds rather than varying smoothly across the neurodevelopmental spectrum. 468 469 

4.4. The Autonomic Axis: Pupillary Synchrony as an Independent Marker 470 Spatial gaze mapping reveals where a child allocates attention, but it cannot capture the 471 physiological intensity of that engagement. Pupillary fluctuations are commonly used as an 472 indirect index of LC-NE-related arousal (20). 473

20 Our multimodal approach revealed that spatial and autonomic entrainment decouple 474 independently. During The Present , the profound reduction in pupil synchrony associated with 475 autistic traits may reflect reduced shared emotional engagement (21). Conversely, the 476 independent decoupling of pupillary synchrony associated with ADHD traits during highly salient 477 media may reflect altered LC-NE-linked arousal regulation. The autonomic axis provides a 478 necessary dimension for clinical phenotyping; relying solely on spatial gaze risks overestimating 479 engagement if the internal physiological state remains decoupled. 480 481 

4.5. Methodological Considerations and RDoC Implications 482 By evaluating physiological synchrony through both continuous traits and discrete diagnostic 483 categories, our findings support the utility of an RDoC-inspired dimensional approach (3). 484 Continuous trait severity smoothly predicted spatiotemporal and autonomic synchrony 485 reductions. However, the discovery of the antagonistic buffering effect in the categorical models 486 cautions against abandoning clinical boundaries entirely. The comorbidity category may capture 487 non-additive interactions that are not fully explained by dimensional traits alone. 488 Several limitations must be considered. First, despite high significance due to the large 489 sample, the standardized effect sizes ( β, ηp2 ) remain small. Their practical utility for individual-490 level diagnostic predictions requires further investigation. Second, the dimensional analyses 491 included a heterogeneous "Other" diagnostic group (28% of the sample), which may have 492 introduced confounding variance into the continuous regressions. Third, an asymmetrical 493 reference design was utilized for ISC calculation, correlating clinical participants against the full, 494 slightly larger neurotypical group, potentially introducing a subtle baseline bias. Finally, while our 495 sensitivity analyses ruled out acute stimulant effects (Section 3.5 / Supplementary Section S3), 496 future research should explicitly test broader pharmacological histories and integrate 497 standardized, clinician-administered assessments (e.g., ADOS-2) to further validate these 498 physiologically derived biomarkers. 499

5. Conclusion 501 This study demonstrates the utility of multimodal naturalistic viewing to capture the physiological 502 landscape of neurodevelopment. By deploying a 2D Complex Correlation framework across a 503 large pediatric cohort, we revealed that physiological decoupling is deeply context-dependent, 504 driven uniquely by social demands in ASD and dynamic salience in ADHD. Furthermore, 505 absolute phase mapping did not support the assumption of a universal spatiotemporal offset, 506 highlighting instead a profoundly idiosyncratic spatial-temporal locking. Crucially, categorical 507 modeling revealed an antagonistic interaction where ADHD-driven hyper-scanning appears to 508 buffer the severe "sticky" attention characteristic of isolated ASD. Ultimately, model-free 509 spatiotemporal and autonomic synchrony offers a promising, scalable approach to understand, 510 phenotype, and support neurodivergent development. 511 512 513
