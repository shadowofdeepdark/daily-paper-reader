# Abstract 11 

Speech perception has been hypothesized to exploit neural activity that entrain s to the 12 

rhythmic properties of the utterance. Perceptual sensitivity varies with the phase of osci llatory 13 

activity induced by speech , and with an external oscillatory perturbation induced by 14 

transcranial alternating current stimulation (tACS) over the temporal lobe. Here we used a15 

new form of non -invasive brain stimulation (NIBS) , kilohertz transcranial magnetic 16 

perturbation (kTMP) , in which a low frequency perturbation is created by the amplitude 17 

modulation (AM) of a continuous high -frequency carrier signal (3.5 kHz) . As a magneti c18 

induction method, kTMP can produce much higher electrical fields at the cortical surface than 19 

tACS and reduces cutaneous co -stimulation. Human participants (n=40) listened to sequences 20 

of rhythmic speech while receiving kTMP with an AM component of 3.125 Hz. The phasic 21 

modulation of perceptual sensitivity was greater during AM -kTMP than during sham 22 

stimulation , suggesting that AM -kTMP entrain s neural activity underlying speech perception .23 

Participants’ ratings of rhythmic sensations and other side effects did not differ between AM -24 

kTMP and sham stimulatio n. Together with its reduced peripheral stimulation, our results 25 

highlight kTMP as a promising NIBS method for modulating frequency -specific brain dynamics 26 

in speech perception .27 

28

# Introduction 32 

The phase of spontaneous brain oscillations can modulate human perception (1 –3) . Rhythmic 33 

stimulation plays an important role in this context, as it provi des a temporal scaffold to shape 34 

neural activity (4) . Stimulus -brain synchronisation is often called “neural entrainment” (5,6) .35 

Due to a remarkable resemblance between the frequency of brain oscillations and rhythms 36 

found in human speech (7,8) , it has been proposed that the entrainment of brain os cillations 37 

is fundamental for speech understanding (9,10) . Indeed, neural entrainment to speech rhythm 38 

becomes stronger when speech is intelligible (11 –13) . Several lines of evidence converge to 39 

suggest that neural entrainment to speech and other stimuli involves brain oscillations (13 –40 

17) . However, a s endogenous brain oscillations are difficult to identify during rhyt hmic 41 

stimulation (8,18,19) , we do not make this assumption in the present study , and use t he term 42 

“neural entrainment” to refer to stimulus -aligne d neural activity (6) .43 

44 

Non -invasive brain stimulation (NIBS) methods have been used to manipulate neural 45 

entrainment and demonstrate d its causal role in various cognitive functions . Varying the 46 

phase of transcranial alternating current stimulation (tACS ) relative to rhythmic sequences of 47 

speech sounds alters speech processing, presumably by modulating the alignment of neural 48 

activity with the speech rhythm (20 –27) . Measures of s peech perception continue to fluctuate 49 

briefly at the stimulation frequency after tACS offset (13,14) . Such sustained rhythmic effects 50 

support an entrainment of endo genous brain oscillations (18) . However, due to tolerability 51 

limits imposed by peripheral nerve stimulation , electric conduction methods, such as tACS, 52 

only produce weak electric fields of ~0.2 -0.5 V/m on the cortical surface at conventional 53 

intensities (1 -2 mA) (28) . Moreover, given that m uch of the applied current is shunted by the 54 

skin, it has been suggested that cutaneous stimulation, rather than direct brain stimulation, 55 

contribute s to the phasic modulation of speech processing (29) .56 

57 

Magnetic induction methods, such as transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) , can produce 58 

stronger cortical stimulation than tACS and have been instrumental in establishing a causal 59 

role of the superior temporal gyrus (STG) in speech processin g (27,30,3 1) . While rhythmic TMS 60 

(rTMS) can entrain neural activity (32) , the TMS pulses are discernible as auditory clicks and 61 

the somatosensory taps associated with each pulse can become uncomfortable when 62 

targeting STG due to activation o f facial muscles (33 –35) . While peripheral sensations can be 63 

matched with appropriate active controls , the rhythmic nature of the sensations may interfere 64 

with that of the speech signal. Moreover, the pulsed stimulation wave form has power in a 65 

wide range of frequencies, limiting the potential for probing frequency -specific effects on 66 

neural activity. 67 

68 

Kilohertz transcranial magnetic perturbation (kTMP) has developed as a n alternative NIBS 69 

method capable of producing subthreshold E -fields that are much higher than that possible 70 

with tACS (36) . kTMP is a magnetic induction method in which an amplifier is used to drive a 71

# Methods 105 

## Participants 106 

40 participants were recruited from the UC Berkeley community. Testing following the 107 

procedures outlined in a protocol approved by the IRB at UC Berkeley. All participants met 108 

inclusion criteria for research involving NIBS methods and provided informed written consent .109 

The participants received $45 for their participation. 110 

111 

## Stimuli 112 

The stimuli were developed in previous studies by our group (23,25,26) . Monosyllabic words 113 

were initially spoken by a male speaker in time with a metronome beat (which was not 114 

presented to participants ) at 1.6 Hz . The recordings were time -compressed offline to 3.125 Hz 115 

using the pitch -synchronous overlap and add (PSOLA) algorithm implemented in the Praat 116 

software (44) . Th is compression ensured that stimulus rate corresponds to one where 117 

auditory sensitivity is relatively high (45) and might therefore produce relatively strong neural 118 

entrainment .119 

120 

To create word sequences for each trial, the five words were combined to form sequences of 121 

rhythmic speech ( Fig . 1A, orange ). The first and the last words were always “pause” , included 122 

to serve as filler items. The middle three words were the target items. They were randomly 123 

selected from the stimulus set and linguistically unrelated to one another. The intelligibility of 124 

the words was reduced by embedding them in noise that spe ctrally matched the average 125 

spectral content of all words (4:1 ratio of root -mean squared sound amplitudes between 126 

speech and noise). The stimuli were presented to participants through insert earphones (ER -127 

1, Etymotics). 128 

129 

## kTMP 130 

The kTMP system (for details, see (37) ) includes an amplifier that drives a coil to induce 131 

magnetic stimulation. In the present study, we used a Gen3 kTMP system (Magnetic Tides Inc., 132 

Berkeley, CA, USA) to drive a  MagVenture Cool B -70 figure -of -eight coil (MagVenture A/S, 133 

Farum, Denmark) . The carrier frequency was set to 3.5 kHz, and this signal was amplitude 134 

modulated at 3.125 Hz (Fig. 1A, blue ). Stimulation was targeted at the  left superior temporal 135 

gyrus (STG) , based on standard MNI coordinates (62 -8 -2). To locate this position, we used 136 

the neuronavigation system Brainsight (Rogue Research, Montreal, Canada ). The coil 137 

orientation was perpendicular to the target region (Fig. 2A ), with the coil handle pointing 138 

upwards . This orientation minimi zed the distance between the coil and the scalp while 139 

avoiding contact with the ear. Coil position was monitored continuously with the Brainsight 140

We took two steps to mask periphe ral sensations associated with kTMP. First, the system 154 

emits sound that contains a frequency component at the carrier frequency of stimulation and 155 

that is amplitude modulated during AM -kTMP . To mask this sound , we created an auditory 156 

mask to match its spectral profile and was played via loudspeakers located behind participants 157 

at approximately 2 0 dB louder than the kTMP -generated sound, corresponding to a 100x 158 

increase in physical intensity and a 4x increase in perceptual loudness . The mask was played 159 

during both AM -kTMP and Sham stimulation. Second, at the target scalp position used here, 160 

a slight vibration can be experienced from the coil during AM -kTMP. To mask this sensation, 161 

a towel was placed between the c oil and the scalp . These procedures were successful in 162 

masking these peripheral sensations, as average sensations (including rhythmic ones) were 163 

consistently rated as weaker than “just noticeable” for both active AM -kTMP and s ham 164 

stimulation , without reliable differences between the two (see Results) .165

## Experimental Paradigm 167 

The experiment consisted of 8 blocks of ~4.2 min utes each. Half of the blocks involved real 168 

AM -kTMP stimulation and the other half involved sham stimulation. Block order was pseudo -169 

randomised with the constraint that each condition occurred equally often across the 170 

experiment .171 

172 

Each block consisted of 32 trials, with a trial defined as one five -word sequence (1.6 s long) 173 

and a subsequent response window . Within each trial, the five words were presented so that 174 

their perceptual centre (4 8) , the part of the word that had been aligned with the metronome 175 

beat by the speaker , occurred at one of eight phases of the AM -kTMP waveform in steps of 176 

45 ° (Fig. 1B ). The phases were selected pseudo -randomly for each trial. Following the 177 

presentation of the sequence, the participant used a keyboard to type the middle three words. 178 

They were encouraged to guess or type in word fractions if unsure. The time window for 179 

participants’ respon ses was always 5. 4 s, followed by an inter -trial interval that was variable 180

## Data analysis 198 

Participants’ perceptual accuracy was quantified using Levenshtein distance (23) , the 199 

minimum number of edits (deletions, insertions) necessary to change the response in to the 200 

target word. To do so, responses and target words were converted into a phonetic 201 

representation using the “Phonemizer ” package (49) . This score was computed for each word 202 

and the score for the three words in a given trial were averaged. Given that the participant 203 

might shuffle the order when entering their responses, we calculated the Levenshtein 204 

distances for each combination of target and response words within a trial and selected the 205 

combination with the highest accuracy. Note that any bias introduced by this procedure will 206 

impact performance on AM -kTMP and sham trials. 207 

208 

We used a regression model to test how strongly the phase of the AM componen t of kTMP 209 

(sine - and cosine -transformed) predicts single -trial measures of performance (50) . For 210 

individual participants, th e model includes an intercept term and two circular predictors (one 211 

for sine, one for cosine predictor) that together reflect the magnitude of phasic modulation. 212 

This approach is equivalent to fitting a sine function to task accuracy as a function of oscill atory 213

# Discussion 342 

We here used a novel transcranial magnetic stimulation technique, AM -kTMP, to modulate 343 

speech perception, putatively through a manipulation of neural entrainment. Our results 344 

indicate that AM -kTMP can modulate neural activity entrained to rhythmic properties of 345 

speech, and complement previous tACS work suggesting that this entrainment causally 346 

modulates speech processing (20 –27,29) .347 

348 

A key drawback of NIBS is its sensory co -stimulation that can be auditory, visual (retinal), or 349 

somatosensory, depending on the protocol used . During tACS, a large portion of the current 350 

is shunted by the skin (51) , which reduces direct brain stimulation and leads to simultaneous 351 

cutaneous stimulation that can affect neural or perceptual outcomes (52) . Previous work 352 

suggested that cut aneous stimulation can indeed contribute to phasic tACS effects in speech 353 

perception (29) . Conventional TMS produces considerable tactile sensations (53,54) as well as 354 

‘click’ sounds that follow the stimulation rate during rTMS. As AM -kTMP applies magnetic 355 

(rather than electric) and continuous (rather than pulse -shaped) stimulation , and the sou nd 356

stimulation and associated sensations ( Fig. 4 ). Sensations reported, including rhythmic ones, 358 

did not differ between AM -kTMP and sham stimulation, replicating a pre vious study (37) .359 

These observations highli ght AM -kTMP as promising method to study the causal role of neural 360 

entrainment in different processes and regions. Nevertheless, the effect size observed here 361 

(Cohen’s d = 0.29 for the contrast between AM -kTMP and sham stimulation) resembles those 362 

typically obtained with tACS (22,23,26) . Although this might seem surprising at first glance, 363 

given the significantly stronger electric field produced by kTMP, there are several points to 364 

consider. 365 

366 

First, AM -kTMP has only been develop ed recently . Consequently, we applied AM -kTMP for 367 

only ~16 minutes per participant , obtain ing a lower number of trial s compared to previous 368 

tACS work. It is likely that longer and stronger stimulation will become routine and improv e369 

statistical power in the future. Dose -response curves need to be established for AM -kTMP. 370 

The fact that p has ic modulation did not differ between 5. 03 V/m and 7.82 V/m indicates that 371 

the curve is relatively flat between these intensities . Due to its relatively young age , optimal 372 

parameters for the use of AM -kTMP to manipulate neural entrainment to speech also remain 373 

unknown – including the question whether the left STG is the ideal target for this purpose .374 

The minimum stimulation intensity to achieve reliable effects in auditory cortical regions is yet 375 

to be established. For the motor and visual systems, such effects are often expressed relative 376 

to available correlates of neural excitability, such as the motor evoked potential or 377 

phosphenes (55,56) , but auditory research lacks an equivalent marker. This makes it difficult 378 

to adjust stimulation intensities to individual response thresholds. 379 

380 

Second , improvements are possible to achieve consistent and efficacious stimulation effects 381 

in the target reg ion . Electric field simulations should be combined with individual structural 382 

MRI scans (57) to extract target locations for individual participants. Cortical folding patterns 383 

(gyrification) represent a key factor of variability in non -inva sive brain stimulation (58 –60) ,384 

possibly amplified by anatomical variation in the STG (61) . The dorsoventral angle of current 385 

injection of our stimulation montage (illustrated in Fig. S2) might be sensitive to such 386 

variation , due to a high number of variability folds and their respective folding angles in the 387 

dorsoventral plane . Moreover, since the magnetically induced electric field is not fixed relative 388 

to the participant’s head , it is subject to variations due to head movement. Improvements in 389 

neuronavigation  hardware and automated repositioning of the induction coil would be helpful 390 

in this regard. 391 

392 

Third, neural entrainment at physiologically relevant frequencies is assumed to be achieved 393 

through kTMP’s AM component. Previous tACS work suggest that entrainment effects are 394 

weaker if they are conveyed through an amplitude modulated carrier as compared to 395 

conventional (unmodulated low -frequency) tACS (41,62,63) . However, u nlike tACS, the kTMP 396 

electric field magnitude can, with hardware development, be safely increased by at least a 397

Several complementary or alternative stimulation protocols are plausible for the use of AM -401

Post -hoc sorting of stimulation phases can be inefficient, and a more targeted stimulation 405

waveforms, making it an interesting candidate method to apply magnetic TIS. Indeed, efforts 420 

to develop such protocols are ongoing (72) .421

# Conclusi on423 

AM -kTMP is a novel form of NIBS that addresses several pressing issues in the field , including 424 

cutaneous co -stimulation and inflexible stimulation waveforms . We found that the phasic 425 

modulation of word report accuracy was stronger during AM -kTMP than during sham 426 

stimulation , suggesting that AM -kTMP en train ed neural activity related to speech perception .427 

Although effect sizes d id not exceed those reported for more established methods, an 428 

optimisation and individualisation of AM -kTMP will likely increase effects further, and can 429 

make AM -kTMP a key method in the field of neural entrainment and its effect on cognition .430
