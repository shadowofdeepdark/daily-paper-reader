17 Abstract 

18 This study investigated how different forms of task influence the oral 

19 performance of Chinese second-language learners and native speakers. By analyzing 

20 data from 40 Chinese second-language learners and 40 native speakers through 

21 picture description tasks, formal and informal questions, and questions with different 

22 emotions (happy and unhappy), it was found that different task characteristics 

23 significantly affected language performance. Short picture tasks led to higher 

24 communication efficiency and noun rates but more errors, while long story tasks 

25 showed higher verb rates, function word rates, etc. Formal questions had more 

26 characters and nouns but lower communication efficiency compared to informal ones. 

27 Also, happy emotion questions resulted in fewer characters, sentences, and errors than 

28 unhappy emotion questions. These findings contribute to the theoretical understanding 

29 of task-based language performance in Chinese as a second language and offer 

30 practical implications for teaching, textbook compilation, and student evaluation. 

31 32 Keywords : Chinese second language, Oral performance, Task-based language 

33 performance, Language parameters

83 2. Review of Literature 

84 Numerous studies have explored the impact of diverse task characteristics on 

85 language production (De Jong et al., 2012; Ellis, 2005; Llompart, 2024; Purpura et al., 

86 2017; Vasylets et al., 2017). Tavakoli (2011) claimed that longer narrative tasks could 

87 lead to more complex language use as learners had to organize and connect a series of 

88 events. In the context of picture description tasks, shorter pictures often prompt 

89 learners to focus on key objects, resulting in a higher noun rate as they quickly 

90 identify and name the elements. This was supported by Gan (2013), who showed that 

91 in short picture tasks, learners could convey core information more rapidly, which is 

92 beneficial for basic vocabulary learning. Robinson (2001) proposed that more 

93 complex tasks, like long story picture description, require learners to allocate more 

94 cognitive resources, thus promoting the development of higher order language skills. 

95 Şendurur and Yildirim (2015) investigated the influence of task type on second 

96 language performance and found that formal tasks generally led to more attention to 

97 form, resulting in more complex language but potentially lower communication 

98 efficiency. In contrast, casual questions, as studied by Duff (2013, 2015), allows for a 

99 more natural and spontaneous language use, with higher communication efficiency. 

100 The role of emotion in language production has also been an area of interest 

101 (Binyamin-Suissa, et al., 2019; Driver, 2022; Golombek & Doran, 2014; Out et al., 

102 2020). Pavlenko (2005) and Thiel, Connelly and Griffith (2012) explored the 

103 relationship between emotion and language, suggesting that emotional state can

214 215 3.3 Methods 

216 All speech corpus materials were accessed for research analysis on 1 January 2024. 

217 No personally identifiable information of participants was accessible to the authors 

218 during and after data collection. All speech recordings were anonymized immediately 

219 following data collection; all names, contact details and institutional labels were 

220 permanently deleted. Upon completion of the recording process, the audio data were 

221 transcribed into text using machine recognition followed by human verification. 

222 Interviews were conducted with the explicit consent of the participants, and the entire 

223 process was recorded. The M4A files were converted to WAV format, and the audio 

224 was then transcribed using web-based speech recognition tools. The textual information, 

225 obtained through transcribing audio recordings via web-based speech recognition tools 

226 (with subsequent human verification to ensure accuracy), was organized numerically 

227 into an Excel spreadsheet. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS. Paired 

228 sample t-test were utilized to assess the impact of different tasks on language 

229 performance. For linguistic analysis, we selected 13 specific measurement parameters, 

230 which are detailed as follows:

232 233 A comprehensive evaluation of participants’ language proficiency and 

234 performance in different environments was conducted using above parameters. The 

235 number of nouns is converted into the noun rate, which is the number of nouns divided 

236 by the number of information words; the number of verbs is converted into the verb 

237 rate, which is the number of verbs divided by the number of information words; the 

238 number of FW is converted into the FW rate, which is the number of FW divided by 

239 the total number of characters; the SL is calculated as the total number of characters 

240 divided by the total number of sentences; the rate of SFP is the number of SFP divided 

241 by the total number of characters; the repetition rate is the number of repetitions divided 

242 by the total number of characters; the error rate is the number of errors divided by the 

243 total number of characters; the hesitation rate is the number of hesitations divided by 

244 the total number of characters; the CE is the total number of information words divided 

245 by the duration in seconds. The fluency of language expression is correlated with 

246 parameters such as the error frequency, the hesitation count, and the repetition 

247 frequency (Guz, 2015; Suzuki et al., 2021; Wang & Wang, 2024). A lack of fluency 

248 begets an augmentation in errors, hesitations, and repetitions. Conversely, an 

249 abundance of errors, hesitations, and repetitions will also curtail the fluency (Coutinho 

250 dos Santos, 2022; Han & Yang, 2023; Mirdamadi & De Jong, 2015; Tavakoli et al., 

251 2020). The communication efficiency is intimately associated with multiple indices

425 426 6. Conclusions 

427 6.1 Implications 

428 The long story task has unique advantages in testing language complexity. 

429 Participants use verbs more frequently and have a greater variety of function word 

430 usage, which helps them practice complex sentence construction and context 

431 adaptation abilities. By telling and retelling long stories, students can gradually master 

432 various intonation expressions and sentence connection methods in Chinese, 

433 improving the coherence and richness of their oral and written expressions (Baralt, et 

434 al., 2014). The long story task prompts students to attempt to construct longer and 

435 complete sentences and learn to correctly use sentence final particles to express 

436 emotions and attitudes. The above findings provide an empirical basis for teaching 

437 Chinese as a second language. Teaching strategies can be adjusted according to 

438 students’ performance in different task types. For example, in the initial stage, more 

439 short picture tasks can be used to train basic vocabulary and sentence patterns. As 

440 students’ proficiency improves, long story tasks can be gradually introduced to 

441 promote the development of higher order comprehensive language application 

442 abilities. At the same time, personalized language correction and expansion training 

443 can be carried out according to the weaknesses of students reflected in different task 

444 types. When compiling textbooks or test papers for teaching Chinese as a second

501 502 6.2 Limitations

507 focus on more complex form of tasks. Using multiple types of task, such as spontaneous 

508 conversations or debates, would offer a more holistic assessment of oral performance 

509 in different communicative scenarios. Additionally, the study was conducted in a 

510 specific educational context, and the findings may not be directly applicable to other
